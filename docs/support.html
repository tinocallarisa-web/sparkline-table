A table for KPI tracking and actual against plan across many rows — stores, sales reps, products, plants. It answers the question of the monthly review: which rows are drifting from plan, and is it a trend or one bad month? Your hierarchy with Power BI's own subtotals, the value, the change against the previous period or last year, and a sparkline on every row. The free version loads every row. Pro adds the variance against plan, prior year or forecast with a bar per row, status icons, up to three sparkline columns, six collection styles, conditional formatting (fx), row images and the horizon sparkline.

Quick Start

  1. Add Sparkline Table by TCViz to the page. With no fields it shows a start screen with these steps.
  2. Drag one to three fields to Rows, outermost first — Region › Sales rep › Store.
  3. Drag a month or date to Period: it becomes the sparkline axis.
  4. Drag one to three measures to Values — Sales first. Add Target for a target line on every sparkline.
  5. Optional: your plan measure in Plan. It shows as a grey column; with Pro, the variance and its bar appear next to it.

While editing, a setup guide in the corner walks through your fields, how to read the table and the style.

How to read it

The value is the last period (or the total of the range); Δ and Δ% compare it with the previous period or the same period last year, with a sign as well as a colour. The sparkline shows the whole range, so a row that is down this month can be read at once as a dip or as a slide. Subtotal rows are calculated by Power BI.

Use cases

Stores against plan

Rows = Region › Store, Period = Month, Values = Sales, Plan = Sales plan. With Pro, ΔPL% and the variance bar show which stores are off plan; the sparkline shows whether it is a trend or one bad month. Subtotal rows are the ones Power BI calculates for each region.

Sales reps month by month

Rows = Area › Sales rep, Values = Sales, Margin %, Orders. The value column shows the last month, Δ% compares with the month before (or with the same month last year). Sort by Δ% with a click on its header to put the biggest drops first.

Products against a target

Rows = Category › Product, Target = the target measure, Sparkline type = Bullet. Each row is compared with its own target; the bands say poor, fair or good, and the label reads “1.54M · 101%”.

Plants and KPIs with status (Pro)

Rows = Plant › Line, Values = Output, Plan = Output plan, Status icons on: ● good, ▲ fair, ◆ poor, by your own thresholds. Up to three sparkline columns show output, scrap rate and downtime side by side.

Long lists

Thousands of rows (stores, customers, SKUs): rows arrive in segments and the table scrolls without redrawing everything. The search box finds a row among the loaded ones and shows it with its path.

Plan in one minute

  1. Drag your plan measure to Plan (and, if you have it, last year to Prior year).
  2. Free: the plan appears as a grey reference column with the figure of the same period as the value.
  3. Pro: ΔPL, a variance bar (right and green when favourable, left and red when not) and ΔPL%, on every row and subtotal. Values → A decrease is good swaps the colours for costs.
  4. Pro: Status icons on — ● good, ▲ fair, ◆ poor, by your thresholds on ΔPL%.

Plan defined only by region? The visual shows “—” below that level instead of a meaningless variance. The FAQ has the DAX to make the measure stop there.

Selection, levels and interaction

  • Click a row to filter the rest of the page. Click the value or the sparkline of a subtotal row to select it: it passes all its rows. Ctrl/Cmd + click adds; click again, click the background or press Esc to clear.
  • Click the arrow or the name of a row to expand or collapse it; Alt + click for the whole level. Level buttons 1 · 2 · 3 in the header open the table to that level.
  • Highlight: when another visual filters this one, rows without highlighted data are faded.
  • Tooltip on the sparkline with the period under the mouse; Rows → Tooltips can show the whole row or nothing. Report page tooltips are supported.
  • Click a column header to sort by it; the search box (Column headers → Search box) finds rows among those loaded, with their path, without filtering the report.
  • Bookmarks restore the selection and the expanded rows. Right-click opens the Power BI context menu.
  • When the report author turns interactions off, clicks do not filter.

Accessibility

  • Keyboard: Tab into the visual; up and down move between rows; PageUp/PageDown and Home/End jump; right expands (or goes to the first child), left collapses (or goes to the parent); Enter or Space selects (Ctrl adds); Esc clears.
  • The focused row is announced to screen readers with its name, level, value, Δ%, status and whether it is expanded or selected.
  • High contrast: the visual uses the colours of the Windows contrast theme; status is told apart by shape, a negative variance bar is hatched and the selection is outlined.
  • Δ and Δ% carry a sign as well as a colour; status icons differ in shape as well as colour.
  • The layout adapts to the size of the visual: when it narrows, the extra columns give way first; the sparkline stays.

Troubleshooting

The plan shows “—” below a certain level

Your plan measure is defined at one level only (for example by region), and Power BI repeats the region's plan on every store under it. With Rows → Repeated references = Hide (default) the visual detects the level where the plan stops being defined and shows “—” from there on, with no variance against it; the header says “Plan: available down to Region”. See the recipe in the FAQ to stop the measure going down.

Rows with only the plan appear

A measure defined at another level of detail returns a value for every row of the dimension, also where nothing was sold. Rows → Rows without actual = Hide (default) hides rows where all Values are blank; the header says how many. Subtotals do not change: they are Power BI's. The FAQ has the DAX fix.

“Same period last year is not available on this axis”

The visual needs to know which period is the same one a year earlier. It recognises dates, 2026-09, 202609, 09-2026, 2026-Q3 / Q3 2026, Sep 2026 and years. With another format it compares with the previous period and says so.

Δ% is empty for a row

The base is zero or negative, so a percentage would not mean anything. The tooltip says so. Δ% is also shown dimmed when the base is very small compared with the rest of its level (Values → Dim Δ% on small bases).

The value column is empty but the sparkline has data

The value column shows the last period of the axis, the same for every row. A row without data in that period has an empty value; its sparkline ends at its own last point, and the label next to it shows that month, dimmed (“1.22K · Jun 2026”).

“n values without a date on the axis are not drawn”

Some rows of your data have a blank period. They are left out of the sparkline, of the header range, of the last period and of Δ. With Value shows the total of the range, the total comes from Power BI and does include them: the header says “Total (incl. no date)”. Check the relationship between your fact table and the calendar.

“Row limit reached: showing N rows”

The visual keeps asking Power BI for more rows until all have arrived. If Power BI stops sending them, the note says how many are shown. Filter the page or collapse a level.

“Total of the range did not arrive from Power BI”

Value shows the total of the range asks Power BI for the total of each row. If it does not arrive, the value shows the last period. Turn the option off and on again, or reduce the number of rows.

A row looks flat on its sparkline

Columns and area start at zero, so a series that moves between 40K and 50K looks almost full: a bar that does not start at zero exaggerates. For the shape, use Line without Start at zero. With Shared by level, rows share one scale; Per row gives each row its own.

“Plan and Sales use different formats”

While editing, the visual warns when the reference and the actual have different number formats (litres against euros, for example): the variance may not make sense. It blocks nothing and is not shown to readers.

Images do not appear

Only Base64 data URIs are drawn (data:image/png;base64,…, also jpeg, gif, webp, bmp and svg+xml). A web address is never loaded; while editing, a note says so. Check also Row levels → Show row image for that level. Row images are part of Pro.

I see a “Pro preview” watermark

A Pro feature is turned on (or Plan / Prior year / Forecast is in its field with the Variance card on) and you are editing without a licence. Turn it off to remove the watermark, or get a licence. Readers of the report without a licence see the free table, with no watermark.

FAQ

My plan exists only by region: why “—” on stores, and how do I stop it going down?

When a plan measure has no detail below region, Power BI cannot split it and repeats the region's figure on every sales rep and store. A variance of −90% on each store against the whole region's plan would mean nothing, so with Rows → Repeated references = Hide the visual shows “—” in grey from the level where the plan stops being defined: no variance, no bar, no status against it. Regions and the total do not change.

To make the measure itself return a value only where it is defined, blank it below its level with ISINSCOPE:

Plan (region only) =
IF (
    ISINSCOPE ( Stores[Sales rep] ) || ISINSCOPE ( Stores[Store] ),
    BLANK (),
    [Sales plan]
)

Use the columns of your own Rows hierarchy. The same works for Prior year, Forecast and Target.

How do I show a flag or logo on each row?

Row images (Pro) take a Base64 data URI, not a web address: the visual has no network access, so an image on a web server is never loaded. That is what keeps the privacy promise literal.

  1. Make a small table Flags with two columns: Country and Image, where Image is text such as data:image/svg+xml;base64,PHN2Zy… or data:image/png;base64,iVBOR….
  2. Create a measure that looks the image up for the country in scope:
Flag =
IF (
    ISINSCOPE ( Geography[Country] ),
    LOOKUPVALUE ( Flags[Image], Flags[Country], SELECTEDVALUE ( Geography[Country] ) )
)
  1. Drag the measure to Row image. Turn images on or off per level in Row levels → Show row image, with size and a square or round shape.

Keep images small (an icon of a few KB); very long text values can be cut by Power BI when the model loads. Rows → Reserve space for row images keeps names aligned when a row has no image.

Why do “phantom” rows with only a plan appear?

A measure at another level of detail than the rows (a plan by region, rows down to sales rep) returns a value for every member of the dimension, also those that sold nothing there. The native matrix does the same with measures that never return BLANK. The visual hides rows where every measure in Values is blank (Rows → Rows without actual = Hide, default), and the header says how many. The fix in the model is to return the plan only where there is actual:

Plan (where there is actual) =
IF ( NOT ISBLANK ( [Sales] ), [Sales plan] )
Why does a row show its last value from an earlier month?

The value column always shows the same period for every row: the last period of the axis. A store that has no data that month has an empty value — the visual never takes an older month without saying so. Its sparkline does end at its real last point, and the label next to it carries that month, dimmed: “1.22K · Jun 2026”. The same applies to sparkline columns 2 and 3 and to horizon.

What happens with an empty period or rows without a date?

A blank period, shown as “(Blank)” by Power BI, is left out of the sparkline axis, of the header range, of the last period and of Δ. While editing, a note says how many values have no date. With Value shows the total of the range the total is Power BI's and does include them; the header says “Total (incl. no date)”. Nothing is subtracted. To get rid of the blank period, fix the relationship between the fact table and the calendar.

Who calculates the subtotals?

Power BI. Each subtotal row is the measure evaluated for that region or sales rep, so Margin % of a region is the region's margin, not the sum or average of its stores. ΔPL of a subtotal is that row's actual minus that row's plan.

Value: last period or total of the range?

By default the value column shows the last period with data. Values → Value shows the total of the range asks Power BI for the total of each row over the whole period axis. Δ and Δ% always refer to the last period (against the previous one or the same one last year); the header says which.

How does the scale of the sparklines work?

Per row in detail (default): each row of the last level has its own scale, and subtotal rows share one scale per level, so a region does not flatten its stores. Shared by level: every row of a level on one scale, to compare sizes. Per row: every row of every level on its own scale, to compare shapes. The header always says which. Horizon and the bullet with a target are always per row.

How do the level buttons and expand/collapse work?

The buttons 1 · 2 · 3 in the header work like Excel's outline levels: 3 opens everything, 2 opens the first level and leaves the second closed, 1 collapses everything. Click the arrow or the name of a row to expand or collapse it; Alt + click opens or closes the whole level. Power BI's own context menu also has Expand / Collapse. Power BI keeps the expanded state, so it is saved with the report and with bookmarks.

How many fields and rows can it use?

Rows up to 3 fields; Period one; Values up to 3 measures; Target, Plan, Prior year, Forecast and Row image one each; Tooltips up to 5. Rows arrive from Power BI in segments of 1,000 and the visual asks for the next segment until all have arrived; while loading, a note says “Loading N of …”. Up to 400 periods are drawn. The limits are the same in the free version and in Pro.

Does sorting change the report?

Click a column header to sort by the figure you see: descending, ascending, then back to Power BI's order, always within each parent. While editing, the sort is saved with the report. When the order comes from Power BI's own Sort by menu, a line under the header says so, because Power BI sorts by the total of the range.

Does it use my number formats?

Yes: the number format of each measure from the model, dynamic format strings included, in the report's language. Δ% and % of target use a percentage format.

Does it follow my report theme?

Yes. Report theme is the default template: colours and text come from your theme, like a native visual. Colours you set yourself always win.

What is the “Pro preview” watermark?

While you edit a report without a licence, Pro features you turn on are drawn working on your own data under a watermark that names them, and Power BI shows its licence notice. Reading view shows the free table: Plan, Prior year and Forecast stay visible as grey reference columns, without variance, status, extra sparklines, styles, fx, images or horizon (a horizon falls back to a line). Where Power BI provides no licence information (for example when you are not signed in to Power BI Desktop), the free table is shown.

How do I get Pro?

On the visual's Microsoft AppSource page, choose Buy now (not Install free). Licences are per user and managed by Microsoft; every person who should see the Pro features needs one. Restart Power BI Desktop after the licence is assigned. See tcviz.com/licensing.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. The visual declares no privileges, so Power BI does not let it make any network request, and it stores nothing outside your report. The links on the start screen open only when you click them, through Power BI.
